# AECO v0.8 — Natural-history bridge: foam nest surface visitors and mating
2026-11-25. This update advances the foam-specific evidence gate, not the general mathematical model. No direct Pieridae use or recipient fertilization is established.

## Evidence recovered
Targeted web searches (English/Japanese): "frog foam nest butterfly feeding Lepidoptera", "foam nest insects feeding", "モリアオガエル 泡巣 チョウ", "泡巣 ハナアブ", "泡巣 交尾 ハエ", "泡巣 交尾 ヤマトシリアゲ", and targeted source-site queries. No systematic all-media corpus or complete denominator. Direct links in the event ledger are source-level candidates; text and video descriptions were accessible, but underlying moving images were not independently coded.

- June 2023: source video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5OeZ0KQJ0nc reports female Episyrphus balteatus landing and licking dry Zhangixalus arboreus foam, alongside an unidentified flesh fly and ants. Mouthpart contact is plausible; ingested substance and nutrients are unknown, and exposed eggs/other material cannot be excluded. No mating endpoint.
- Early June 2024: https://sigma-nature-vlog.blogspot.com/2025/06/blog-post_29.html reports male and female Panorpa japonica on one arboreal foam nest, mouthpart contact, wing displays and a copulating pair. Related video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rJqDgQJt-kI and time-lapse archive https://sigma-nature-vlog.blogspot.com/2025_06_15_archive.html belong to the same source series and are grouped, not independent replications. A report of a mating pair on the nest is evidence of reported use as a copulation substrate, NOT causal mating-site formation, a newly initiated copulation, sperm transfer or fertilization. The feeding target (eggs vs foam vs other material) is inferred in the narrative; no dissection or chemical test.
- Independent 2023 natural-history blog https://nojigiku450.blog.fc2.com/blog-entry-1247.html reports scorpionflies gathering on a Japanese treefrog foam nest. Species and ingestion target not independently verified. No mating reported. This is independent source support for gathering, not replication of the mating event.
- Older fly reports (https://sigma-nature-vlog.blogspot.com/2013/04/blog-post_14.html and https://sigma-nature-vlog.blogspot.com/2012/08/blog-post_64.html) show a candidate contrast of egg-exposed and intact nests, but dates and overlap are ambiguous. Treat as one source cluster, no effect estimate.
- Scientific context: the peer-reviewed study of Caiusa flies (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6160781/) documents larval predation associated with foam nests of other frogs. Not a butterfly or adult mating study.

## Mechanistic positive comparator (not a substitute for Pieridae)
Ishihara & Miyatake 2020 https://doi.org/10.1007/s10164-020-00645-3 distinguishes nuptial gifting, mating during female feeding, and forced mating in Panorpa japonica. Ishihara & Miyatake 2021 https://doi.org/10.1007/s10164-021-00709-y documents wing displays and mating-related resource use. Some wing displays also occur in competition; do not code display alone as courtship. Ishihara 2022 dissertation Chapter 2 Fig. 2.3 and prose pp.13–14 (https://ousar.lib.okayama-u.ac.jp/files/public/6/63652/20220616113237654504/K0006645_fulltext.pdf) reports 40 laboratory pairs: bait + pheromone 15/21 mated; approached feeding female 12/13; approached nonfeeding female 4/4; pheromone without female or bait 0/2. Recomputed 27/31 observed matings (87.1%) involved bait-associated branches; 27/40 all pairs (67.5%). The experiment contained bait, not randomized foam vs control; observed behavior categories are post-exposure, not randomized. No causal risk ratio or fertility claim. These counts are a separate experiment, not additional foam observations. PDF screenshot failed 429, but extracted text and Fig. 2.3 agree. Related Panorpa cognata research https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1420-9101.2003.00613.x finds sperm transfer related to mating duration and condition; this is not P. japonica or a foam test.

## New falsifiable comparison
Primary remains Z. arboreus x Pieris; Panorpa is a positive *candidate* comparator for resource-associated mating, E. balteatus a resource contact comparator. Hypothesize that a recipient's resource-linked mating tactic determines whether frog reproductive architecture is merely visited or becomes a mating substrate. In scorpionflies, male food defense/pheromone and encounters with feeding females are supported by unrelated experiments. In Pieridae, visually oriented mate search and male-biased resource use are competing routes; do not transfer the scorpionfly mechanism to butterflies.

Stage A: seek permission for original unedited footage or time-stamped clips from original observer; do not re-upload third-party video or precise locality. Review two independent coders (consumer taxon/sex, foam stage, egg exposure, encounter sequence, genital contact, departure) and adjudicate disagreements. If video starts with a coupled pair, code copulation underway, initiation unknown. No absence inference from edited clips.
Stage B: prospectively sample matched natural intact, egg-exposed/damaged and collapsed foam stages with neighboring control leaves of comparable geometry, height, water proximity, light and host/nectar availability, without touching or changing active nests. Nest damage is endogenous; do not interpret observational stage contrasts as causal. Full daylight blocks plus calibrated cameras, matched detection volumes, background transects, independent ponds and review. Separate taxa in all denominators. Record female arrivals, feeding, male resource defense, newly initiated matings, completed matings and effort. Measure frog egg loss separately only using authorized noninvasive observation. A positive mating report is not evidence that the frog benefits.
Stage C: in permitted contained experiments, independently randomize visual/chemical cues and concentrated vs dispersed equal resource layouts; include matched non-frog food controls. Use safe measured mimics, no active nest sampling or wildlife relocation. Test whether resource-dependent mating tactics mediate actual whole-array mating/confirmed fertilization coverage, and whether the recipient taxon modifies effect. The primary endpoint remains direct fertilization per initially virgin female as in v0.2; hatch, copulation and spermathecal sperm distinct. If no Pieridae visitation with adequate detection, report the negative target result rather than replace it with Panorpa.

## Evidence status
Frog foam surface association and non-pierid copulation on foam: natural-history reports with linked source material, not independently verified video frames. Independent scorpionfly aggregation report: yes. Causal foam-mediated mating or direct recipient fertilization: no. Adult Pieridae foam use: not identified. No new iNaturalist occurrence retrieval or original videos/individual raw data in this update. Focus on authentic behavioral evidence before a co-occurrence analysis.
