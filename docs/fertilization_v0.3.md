# AECO v0.3: fertilization coverage and the movement-dependent break-even threshold

2026-11-25. **Mathematical predictions only.** No frog-derived cue, spatial tracking, or direct fertilization dataset has been acquired.

## Model and theorem
Let H_i be the cumulative hazard of the first completed compatible mating along a female's pre-mating path over the fixed horizon. Under an exogenous path, Poisson conditional hazard, first mating only, and a common conditional probability q of at least one confirmed fertilized egg, expected female coverage is C=q E[1-exp(-H_i)]. Jensen's inequality gives C <= q(1-exp(-E H_i)), with equality only for equal cumulative hazards (q>0). Consequently, more mean potential encounters need not mean greater coverage. It is not a general theorem about mating counts, sperm competition, or lifetime fecundity. If the mean potential hazard is unchanged, heterogeneity cannot improve coverage relative to homogeneous exposure.

K=10 equal patches; male share m in one hotspot, remaining males distributed uniformly. The baseline uniform cumulative hazard is h. Hotspot hazard a=Kmh and outside hazard b=K(1-m)h/(K-1). Female hotspot fraction f. For stationary nonmoving females, C=q[f(1-exp(-a))+(1-f)(1-exp(-b))]. The break-even fraction f*=(exp(-b)-exp(-h))/(exp(-b)-exp(-a)), m>1/K. The mean potential encounter break-even is f=1/K; f* is strictly greater than 1/K for h>0 and heterogeneous male density. This follows from strict concavity and monotonicity in f. For h approaching zero, f* approaches 1/K. For h approaching infinity with b>0, f* approaches one; the absolute difference in coverage nevertheless tends to zero. This is a threshold for the fraction of females using the hotspot, not a fraction of females that copulate there.

## Female mobility
Two-state continuous-time Markov model: hotspot -> outside at rate r(1-f); outside -> hotspot at rate rf. Females start from stationary distribution (f,1-f). Male locations are fixed; r is dimensionless relaxation rate over the fixed horizon, not a count of observed flights. Outside is exchangeable. Before the first completed mating, the row-vector transient generator is [[-a-r(1-f), r(1-f)],[rf,-b-rf]]. Coverage C=q[1-p exp(M) 1]. Fast switching approaches the uniform-per-female exposure q[1-exp(-(fa+(1-f)b))]; the break-even tends to 1/K. Finite switching preserves the Jensen bound. The 100 deterministic cases span 5 male fractions, 4 hazards, and 5 switching rates. A separate scipy matrix exponential calculation cross-checked selected values for m=.6, h=.05,.5,2 and r=0,.1,1,10,100; the closed form was checked against one of those reference thresholds and all 100 break-even roots and Jensen inequalities.

Illustration: m=.6, h=.5, f=.2. The potential encounter hazard ratio is 1.5556 regardless of movement at stationarity. With no movement, coverage ratio=.8881 (11.2% lower); with r=1, .9917; with r=10, 1.2562 (25.6% higher). Break-even f*=.2586, .2037, .1225 respectively. Parameters are not estimated from a species. Rapid mixing does not establish that females will be attracted in the first place.

## Literature constraints
- Cuddington et al. 2015, https://doi.org/10.1007/s12080-015-0254-z : landmark aggregation can reduce mate-finding Allee thresholds; landmark theory is not new. AECO targets asymmetry, integrated individual hazard, and realized fertilization, with an explicit heterospecific provider.
- Bergman et al. 2011, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anbehav.2010.10.009 : virgin Pararge aegeria females changed flight activity with mating status and age, but did not specifically locate the large male sunspot. Female attraction cannot be assumed.
- Hirota & Obara 2003, https://doi.org/10.18984/lepid.54.2_111 : Japanese Pieris rapae males with mean 1.8 days since previous mating searched for females less frequently than virgins. Effective male hazard should account for mating history and recovery; not simply headcount.
- Rutagarama et al. 2023, https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.9956 : 250 Kenyan and 102 Rwandan female Danaus chrysippus dissections; only 10–20% remained unmated despite varying sampled sex ratios. Male proportion was not a significant predictor of mating status (reported p=.171) or spermatophore count (p=.318). This is a constraint on universal mate-limitation claims, not evidence about Pieris, frog cues, absolute male density, or fertilization.
- Pembury Smith et al. 2025, https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.70026 ; data https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.dfn2z35cj : controlled thermal treatment shows mating and hatch-based performance can decouple. Not an encounter-site test.
