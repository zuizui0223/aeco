# AECO v1.3 — Global replication and acquisition gate (2026-11-25)
Primary goal: global remote-sensing-informed analysis of potential cross-taxon reproductive opportunities, not direct detection of centimeter-sized nests. Existing v1.2 workbook is not yet obtained; no global gridded estimate in this document.

## Independently identified global reference
Pereira et al. 2017, https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blx110, open PDF https://repositorio.bc.ufg.br//bitstreams/10d79f73-8c4a-49d4-857f-f8dc605971ea/download . They report 347 foam-nesting species in six families and four continents. Published historical clade summaries: Leptodactylidae 81% of 202 species, Limnodynastidae 70% of 43, Rhacophoridae 35% of 393, Cophylinae 23% of 70. Rounded percentages are not exact counts and the four clades are not the complete six-family list. The phylogeny shows strong conservatism and separate origins in South America, Africa–Asia, Australia–New Guinea and Madagascar; do not treat species as independent evidence of adaptation. The historical family limits (notably Limnodynastidae) and foam definition need review against a current backbone; not all source-family members are foam builders. Cophylinae foam definitions require special scrutiny. Paper p.817–818 figures verified via page screenshots. Table S1 species list identified, not acquired from OUP; no claim that all 347 species are assembled.

Dufresnes et al. (online 2021/issue 2022), https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blab143, reports 30/36 sampled Zhangixalus species with nesting location information, 12/30 (40%) with multiple location types, and no significant elevation/latitude differences among nest-type categories. Their Appendix S2 is a priority source, not downloaded. These results directly challenge fixed binary exposed/buried labels and an assumed monotonic climate/placement relationship. The v1.3 seed is a nonrandom set of 18 explicitly named species from the sources listed in the CSV, not an estimate of global frequency. Region labels are broad study or native-region descriptors, not range polygons; placement classifications are reported possibilities, not a complete species-level inventory. Mixed and unknown retained. All names are as used in source or later literature, not yet validated against an accepted taxonomic backbone. Some source definitions differ from 2021 froth vs bubble classification. 
Pereira et al. 2015 https://doi.org/10.1186/s12862-015-0365-6 provides reproductive mode plus clutch size/habitat data for 35 Leptodactylinae. Additional file 2 is verified by the publisher link but the DOCX was not retrievable through web; mirror Dryad https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.88kj7. Old mode numbers 11/13/30/32 are **not** the same as Nunes-de-Almeida 2021 codes. Do not interpret ancestral pie charts as measured species traits. Distinct South American evidence complements Zhangixalus. The 2022 Rhacophoridae climate study https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-022-03292-1 already compares climates among reproductive modes, limiting novelty of general climate analyses.

## Acquisition and macro analysis
Independent acquisition workflow runs on PR with read-only permissions; attempts exact 2021 workbook (verified publisher file listing, 572950 bytes listed at time of source publication) and 2015 DOCX. Source schema, bytes, hash, status and explicit failures output; no source workbook redistribution. A separate bounded seed GBIF taxonomic and occurrence-facet audit uses 2010–2021, georeferenced present records without reported geospatial issues. No credentials required. Taxon matching must be exact and family-consistent; synonyms and unresolved names retained for audit, duplicated keys must be checked before downstream use. Occurrence counts and country/month facets not reproductive observations, not a spatial joint distribution, and not habitat or phenology estimates. Never treat failed fetches as zero. GBIF API access from the container failed DNS in this turn, as did direct workbook and GitHub raw access; web XLSX and DOCX retrieval report unsupported content types. No initial empirical global result should be claimed from this workflow until artifacts/logs are verified.

FrogID v7 (https://portal.frogid.net.au/science/map-and-data/) advertises >1 million expert-confirmed calls of 228 species for 2017–2024; GBIF dataset v6 DOI 10.15468/wazqft has 974,120 records/226 species as published and explicit calling behavior. This is an important Australian regional validation, not the global primary endpoint and not actual nest production. Check version, license (GBIF v6 CC BY-NC), privacy/coordinate uncertainty, day-level effort and existing frogcs provenance before reuse. No new FrogID data acquired here. 
Once normalized traits and adequate presence/absence or occurrence support are obtained, map exposed, sheltered, mixed and unknown separately on 100km equal-area cells. Use within-family/realm contrasts and independent regions; butterfly target Pieridae, comparison families; analyze trait-specific residual temporal associations beyond rainfall/water/vegetation/observation effort, and lag nulls. Freeze trait scheme before recipient analysis, separate broad range overlap from observed activity. Do not estimate actual recipient fertilization absent appropriate outcomes. If direct acquisition succeeds, first inspect schema and mode definitions; no silent automatic classification or positive-only comparison. No global remote-sensing or recipient data acquired in v1.3 yet.
