# AECO v2.3 — visual ecology of frog-built reproductive structures (2026-11-26 JST)

## Why the new question
User requested heterospecific visual effects rather than another global climatic niche fit. Previous thermal results and negative/underpowered butterfly phenology results remain intact. A foam nest can be a visible *cue* without having evolved as an interspecific signal. Distinguish detection, approach, acceptance, feeding/oviposition/courtship, and subsequent reproductive outcomes.

## Evidence and statistical audit
- Martin et al. 2011, DOI 10.1670/10-006.1: published visible-nest choice 16:8, concealed 12:12, among responding conspecific female tungara frogs choosing calls. Published p=.032 and .419; recomputed conventional exact binomial two-sided p=.15159 and 1.0; one-sided p=.07579 for visible. Independent-cohort Fisher diagnostic p=.38013 (independence not established from aggregate counts). Printed p values do not reproduce under this test; not strong evidence for a visibility interaction. No heterospecific or fertilization effect measured.
- Lin et al. 2000, DOI 10.6660/TESFE.2000027: Caiusa coomani approached an egg-white foam-like mimic in three of five trials, no oviposition; real fresh foam was chosen for oviposition in five. Visual resemblance and chemical/physical factors not isolated. Already captured in v0.9, not new independent evidence. PDF page screenshot attempts failed for both papers; used accessible text/figure extraction.
- Oke et al. 2008, DOI 10.1002/anie.200802901: some Polypedates leucomystax foam becomes blue-green over hours to days, associated with ranasmurfin. Purified-protein absorbance peaks are not whole-nest reflectance. Camouflage, photoprotection and other functions remain hypotheses.
- Beck et al. 1999, DOI 10.1007/s004420050770: conspicuous Eurema decoys attracted papilionid and pierid visitors to puddling baits more than cryptic decoys; lycaenids did not show the same response. Not a foam manipulation; do not extrapolate to all butterflies.

## Next empirical analysis
1. Build a taxonomically and geographically balanced photo set of genuine nests, nonfoam egg masses, and matched natural background; audit duplicates and developmental stage. Within-image RGB contrast is screening only.
2. Model detectability separately from attraction, with calibrated 300–700 nm reflectance, illumination, angular size and receiver-specific receptor sensitivities. Explicitly separate vegetation, soil, water and red/brown leaf backgrounds; do not infer UV from RGB.
3. Test visual-only inert replica (geometry held equal) vs background-matched replica, odor-only vs matched visual cue, and their interaction. Use appropriate receiver groups (frogfly, butterflies, other insects, predators); distinguish approaches, landings, feeding and reproduction; no actual nest manipulation without relevant permissions.
4. Link to climate and vegetation only as modifiers of visual context and persistence after empirical validation, not as evidence of visual behavior or fertility. Retain the previous primary nonreplications. No confirmed visual response of Pieridae to foam or enhanced heterospecific fertilization. Draft PR #9 unmerged.
