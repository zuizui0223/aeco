# AECO v0.6 — Foam nests as heterospecific reproductive architecture
2026-11-25. Foam nests are now the focal provider, rather than frog carrion or generalized frog density. No confirmed adult Pieridae visit, mating or fertilization at a foam nest found in the targeted searches. This is a prospective mechanism hypothesis and a descriptive reanalysis of published frog-nest data, not a verified interspecific effect.

## Mechanistic question
Does a frog-built reproductive structure create a transient landmark/resource patch that changes the distribution of compatible encounters and confirmed fertilization among butterflies? The constructed foam is the provider's extended reproductive phenotype. The primary Japanese system is Zhangixalus arboreus and local Pieris species. Foam is an incubation medium for externally fertilized frog eggs; butterflies have internal fertilization, so do not claim that foam directly transfers butterfly gametes. A useful comparison with plant reproductive ecology is the external construction of spatial opportunities for reproduction, not physiological equivalence.

## Evidence, with limits
- Kusano et al. 2006, Herpetological Journal 16:163–169, https://www.thebhs.org/publications/the-herpetological-journal/volume-16-number-2-april-2006/434-07-ecological-functions-of-the-foam-nests-of-the-japanese-treefrog-i-rhacophorus-arboreus-i-amphibia-rhacophoridae : thermal buffering up to 6 C in hot weather, hatchling nutrition demonstrated. Internal foam temperature is not the thermal environment experienced by visiting butterflies.
- Shigeri et al. 2021, https://doi.org/10.2108/zs200113 : 22 proteins identified by homology in Japanese forest green tree frog foam, including candidate protective functions. Amino acid composition of protein is not free amino acid availability; physiological activity is not established for every homologue. Na concentration and UV spectrum were not identified in the sources reviewed. Inagaki et al. 2024, https://katosei.jsbba.or.jp/view_html.php?aid=1804 provides a Japanese overview.
- Fleming et al. 2009, https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2008.1939 : specialized surfactant protein in Engystomops pustulosus foam did not damage egg/sperm membranes. Distinct species; not a direct test that foam enhances frog fertilization probability, and not a butterfly assay.
- Monteiro et al. 2023, https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms11040900 and https://doi.org/10.1186/s42523-021-00147-8 : foam microbial assemblages in other frog systems; motivate time-dependent chemistry, but do not establish decomposition attraction or microbial succession in Japanese nests.
- Villa et al. 1982, https://repository.si.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/4b906a99-1b43-4a8b-aedb-960473577763/content : arthropod predation/oviposition in and around frog foam nests; distinct from adult butterfly resource use.
- Inoue & Tsuji 2016, https://doi.org/10.2354/psj.32.004 : macaque consumed egg mass together with foam. Ichioka & Hijii 2021, https://doi.org/10.5358/hsj.40.129 : raccoon predation. Ichioka & Kajimura 2024, https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.10926 : maggots present in one dissected arboreal experimental nest with low hatch rate; presence alone does not identify whether larvae fed on foam, eggs, or dead tissues.
- Obara & Hidaka 1968, https://doi.org/10.2183/pjab1945.44.829 : UV wing reflection participates in Japanese P. rapae mate recognition. Flower-visiting study, https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F10855318 : white model visits outdoors included proboscis extension, no copulatory behavior; model UV spectrum differed from female wing. Thus whiteness is not evidence of female mimicry or courtship.
- Boggs & Dau 2004, v0.5 benchmark : low sodium concentrations and sex-resolved substrate visits motivate resource assays; no foam test. Do not assume nitrogen-rich proteins suffice.

## Recalculated baseline
Natural nesting records (Ichioka & Hijii 2021, https://doi.org/10.5358/hsj.40.151): 2019 50 arboreal/15 terrestrial; 2020 65 arboreal/2 terrestrial; 17/132 = 12.88% pooled terrestrial; yearly shares 23.08% and 2.99%. Four ponds repeated; descriptive only, no independent year or nest-level causal estimate. Paper reports many ground nests disappeared/deteriorated prehatching, often predation; arboreal drying vulnerability. Ground position is not randomly assigned in natural data; light, vegetation, moisture, predators and accessibility confound butterfly outcomes.
Placement experiment (Ichioka & Kajimura 2024): Table 1 ten experimental nests, nine reported hatching endpoints, one predation loss. Discovery-to-reported-hatching interval 8–24 days, median 13; excluding three uncertain construction dates, 10–24 days, median 13 (n=6). Operational hatching endpoint corresponds to monitoring nest collapse/hatchling movement; not a measured period of post-hatching residue. Table recording dates for 34/35 start before listed discovery; retain as printed. Natural and experimental nests must not be pooled. Article provides data DOI https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.23667387; DOI and API access failed, direct container DNS also failed. No raw temperature series or new butterfly observations analyzed.

## Rival mechanisms and falsifiable predictions
H_visual: fresh high-contrast foam is an exploratory landmark. Predict early approach to UV/visible matched inert foam replicas vs background-matched replicas with identical chemistry and shape, regardless of feeding. Measure 300–700 nm calibrated reflectance of intact noncontact surfaces, female/male wing reference spectra, backgrounds and illumination; compute receptor-model contrasts only with validated species sensitivities. Human-white or RGB cannot establish resemblance in butterfly vision. Size/shape/position and motion controlled. Static foam cannot be assumed to imitate moving wings. Approaches may represent mate inspection, floral search or neutral exploration; distinguish.
H_resource: accessible moisture/ions/free sugars/free amino acids and odor alter landing/feeding and residency. Predict contact/feeding and dwell differences in identical visual settings with measured chemical cues vs wet and ion-matched controls. Test naturally abandoned foam only when authorized; noninvasive observation first. Internal water chemistry/total protein do not substitute for surface/accessible leachate chemistry. Avoid deliberate exposure to novel chemicals at natural ponds.
H_age: fresh nest visual cue, later breakdown/moist residue chemical cue; ages may instead show no effect or decline in both. Predict temporally different approach vs feeding profiles associated with independently measured cues, not presupposed stage-specific chemistry. Date and rain, dew, adult frog activity, local butterfly phenology and light confound temporal patterns.
H_social: initial arrival recruits others. Randomize presence of realistic stationary butterfly decoys independently of inert nest visual treatment and chemistry. Treat decoy attraction as exploration/aggregation, not female receptivity or mating.
H_trap: visually attractive but unproductive site diverts search, or predators/harassment suppress encounters. Predict more approaches without more completed local matings or confirmed fertilization, or lower whole-array success. Separate neutral/negative results from low power.
H_fertility: among initially unmated females, a concentrated foam-related cue increases the proportion producing at least one demonstrably fertilized egg over a fixed horizon relative to dispersed equal resource or control. This is the final primary endpoint, not a necessary implication of increased visitation. Track array-wide success, mating latency, insemination, fertilized eggs, hatching separately. Distinguish spatial from ingestion effects via standardized pairing/intake treatment in a permitted contained study.

## Three-stage empirical plan
1. During the local frog nesting season (usually May–July), noninvasive matched camera observation of naturally arboreal and terrestrial nests, naturally collapsed remnants/leachate contact surfaces, and no-nest matched controls. Monitor before nest formation where feasible and after collapse, recording observed stages rather than arbitrary ages. Use equal daylight observation effort in matched time blocks; nest/pond is a cluster; repeated frames are not independent. Record actual nest presence, height, stage, incident light, wind, humidity, rain, host plants/nectar, accessible substrate, adult frog observations, background butterfly activity. No collection, displacement, touching active egg masses or visits that risk trampling. Obtain local permissions. Distinguish approaches, passing, landing, proboscis contact, sustained feeding, courtship and copulation initiation. Assign sex only with sufficient evidence; unknown allowed. Positive contact is not proof of ingestion. Null results with effort and detectability. Camera-based behavior classification requires manual audit; no reliance on ordinary co-occurrence.
2. In a controlled, permitted environment independent of active frog nests, cross calibrated visible/UV appearance (matched fresh-foam visual cue versus background control) with chemistry (measured cue mimic versus matched moist control). Use equal size, shape, height and accessibility; separately test spent-foam appearance and chemical fractions if warranted. Randomize locations, balance order, prevent cross contamination. Verify treatments with spectrometer and chemistry. Compare approaches, contact, feeding duration, unique males and females; compare first-visit latency and repeat visits. No frog or nest translocation. Materials from natural nests only after documented natural abandonment, permits and biosecurity; otherwise synthetic measured mimics. If chemistry data unavailable, do not label a generic salt treatment as frog foam.
3. Proceed only after convincing adult Pieridae use or landmark response, sufficient independent replicates and evidence of compatible encounters. Use whole-array concentrated vs dispersed equal cue, track initially virgin females, confirmed fertilization with validated assay/paternity, and explicitly report missing follow-up, no eggs, no mating and mortality. A local increase in copulations may simply redistribute them. Use v0.2–v0.4 models as conditional theory; do not estimate parameters from visitor sex ratios. Quantify frog reproductive costs separately if studying net interactions; no assumption of mutualism.

## Current inference
There is established frog foam-nest architecture, chemistry, function and insect association; the target foam-to-Pieridae connection is unverified. No direct sodium/UV measurements of target nests, no actual butterfly aggregation or fertilization observations. The immediate next gate is direct behavior at foam or controlled response to its verified sensory attributes. Raw Figshare data remain blocked; published table arithmetic is fully recorded. Do not expand to carrion or generic macro co-occurrence unless requested.
