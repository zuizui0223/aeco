# AECO v0.9 — stage-dependent use of frog-built reproductive structures
2026-11-25. Published aggregate benchmark and mechanism refinement. Primary recipient remains adult Pieris; Panorpa remains candidate mating comparator. No direct foam-to-Pieridae visitation or recipient fertilization verified.

## Independent experimental bridge
Lin, Kuo & Lue (2000), Formosan Entomologist 20:281–292, https://doi.org/10.6660/TESFE.2000027. Full PDF https://entsocjournal.yabee.com.tw/AlldataPos/JournalPos/Vol20/No4/TESFE.2000027.PDF. Methods and results pp.282–288, Table 1, Fig. 2–4; text checked against rendered pages for tables/figures, but some Chinese body text did not render in screenshots.
In northern Taiwan, female Caiusa coomani (taxon as published) selected foam nests of Rhacophorus prasinatus and Polypedates megacephalus (host names as published). Three separate paired-choice assays each had five repeats: fresh nest versus whipped egg white, rotten beef, or previously infested nest. Fresh nest was selected for oviposition in all five trials within each comparison. Whipped egg white was approached/hovered over in three trials but did not elicit oviposition. The alternatives differed in more than visual cues. No claim of pure optical causation, independent fly identities, or a randomized trial effect size. This is insect oviposition, not mating, insemination, or recipient fertilization. Supports separating search, approach and acceptance experimentally. A similar chemical/visual separation in Pieris remains an untested cross-taxon prediction.
Females were observed ovipositing during a dawn window (05:00–08:00 in this study); the nest crust was reported moistened overnight and hardened during the day. The reported six oviposition bouts lasted 4–35 min, recalculated mean 14.333 and median 13. One 24-hour survey and four dawn surveys are not a complete circadian sample. Day/night humidity and access covary with time; moisture mediation proposed rather than isolated experimentally.

## Provider cost and stage
Among 20 infested nests, 12 were infested at Gosner 7–8 and 5 at 15–16 (17/20 before stage 17); 3 at 17–18. The corresponding reported nest mean embryo mortality was 100%, 100%, 50%, giving 92.5% across 20 infested nests. 29 uninfested nests averaged 5.3%; descriptive difference 87.2 percentage points, ratio 17.45. No egg-level counts or independent site-level estimates; selection/stage and sample handling differ. This does not estimate an experimentally identified treatment effect, stage-specific infestation risk, or the cost of butterfly visitation. Do not conflate frog embryonic survival with recipient fertilization.

## Japan: measured natural history anchors for residue phase
Toda (2014) doctoral thesis p.45, https://kanazawa-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/35946/files/Full-N-toda-mitsuhiko.pdf, printed page 45 verified by PDF screenshot. Z. arboreus embryos developed and larvae dropped after 7–10 days; dry nests remained at the oviposition site for a further 2–3 weeks. Arithmetic yields 21–31 days visible duration and a possible 58.3–75% residue fraction by combining endpoints. Neither is a measured distribution, resource-use duration, nor a universal estimate. This corrects the prior statement that postdeparture persistence was entirely uncharacterized. At this historical site, late June counts related to seasonal totals (n=23, slope 2.3058, reported R²=.8637); use late June to enrich sampling, not extrapolate seasonal counts with this calibration without validation. Both active and old nests can coexist. Never assume an apparently dry nest is empty.
A separate Japanese comparison candidate is Z. schlegelii, normally underground or at sheltered wet margins, whereas Z. arboreus nests are frequently exposed (https://www.cgr.mlit.go.jp/ootagawa/Bio/amphi/index428.htm; https://www.town.hakone.kanagawa.jp/www/contents/1100000001032/index.html). A 2026 natural-history blog notes few insects at a suspected ground-level nest (https://sigma-nature-vlog.blogspot.com/2026/10/2_0105094505.html), but explicitly doubts its frog identification. Exclude from species-coded comparative analysis; not independent of the v0.8 observer series and not an effort-standardized negative record. Start with within-Z. arboreus natural placement differences and independently measured visibility/access; do not disturb underground nests.

## Revised hypotheses and field plan
H-stage: externally accessible structures outlast frog embryo occupancy. Fresh, intact nests may be found by appearance, egg-associated predators may use an early stage, and verified naturally abandoned remnants may retain visual or chemical cues without direct egg predation. All recipient effects are conditional and may be absent or negative. Test opposite patterns; no assumption of monotonically increasing attraction, of nutrient availability, or of complete abandonment from appearance.
H-diel: surface wetness alters resource access independently of appearance. Use concurrent paired recording from local dawn through daytime including morning and afternoon; no artificial lighting, wetting, or intervention on active nests. Measure ambient RH, surface wetness with noncontact proxies, light and temperature; record wet/dry appearance, precipitation, stage, accessibility and background target activity. Do not import Taiwan clock times or physiological parameters to Japan.
H-layout: visible versus sheltered geometry affects approaches, whereas resource composition predicts contact and residence. Compare matched natural branches and non-nest controls within ponds; record stage changes on the same nests. Stage/placement endogenous; descriptive differences only. Follow naturally departed nests until disappearance where permission allows; use at least 21 days planned follow-up after confirmed departure if feasible, recording missing blocks and right-censoring. No egg removal, handling, collecting active nests, translocation, or wildlife manipulation.
Retain v0.6 factorial contained experiment using noninvasive measured mimics and independently randomized appearance and chemistry after direct target engagement. An approach is not courtship, and observing copulation is not fertilization. Final endpoint: confirmed fertilized egg per initially unmated female, as v0.2. A positive fly oviposition or scorpionfly mating endpoint cannot substitute for a Pieris result.
