# AECO v0.5 — direct frog–butterfly evidence and a substrate-specific empirical benchmark
2026-11-25. Literature aggregate analysis and event ledger; no new raw field observations. Do not infer a frog-mediated mating or fertilization effect.

## Direct link, stronger than v0.4
Downes (1973), Journal of the Lepidopterists' Society 27:89–99 (especially pp.90–91), https://images.peabody.yale.edu/lepsoc/jls/1970s/1973/1973-27%282%2989-Downes.pdf : qualitative addition of horse dung and vertebrate carrion to puddles increased visitor numbers; six butterfly species were collected from a frog carcass, from Lycaenidae and Nymphalidae. Pieris rapae and Colias philodice were documented at ordinary puddles, not among the named frog-carcass specimens. This is evidence for actual frog-derived resource use by some butterflies, NOT a randomized frog-specific effect or an absence test for Pieridae. The original PDF text was inspected, screenshot retrieval timed out. No exact location or image reproduced.

Additional source-level event records: multiple Apatura iris and A. ilia at a Rana temporaria carcass in Ukraine (2010, Wikimedia source); Cethosia biblis on frog carcass in Thailand (published 2018, Cannon natural history blog; one event, not new photo adjudication). The ledger treats photographs of the same event as a single source-level event and distinguishes named taxa from event counts. Three positive-use source records, none confirmed adult Pieridae. Not an exhaustive count; no inference of population prevalence. Babu et al. 2024 is a separate published negative interaction (live frog predation on Papilio polytes in an aggregation), not a positive subsidy.

## Empirical bridge: Boggs & Dau 2004
Source: https://academic.oup.com/ee/article/33/4/1020/449418 ; Tables 1, 4, 5, text. PDF screenshots failed; table values cross-checked using publisher-indexed text and the ResearchGate full-text transcription. The focal Colorado taxon is named Pieris napi by the authors; modern taxonomic assignment not resolved. Do not treat it as a confirmed same-species Japanese comparator.
- Natural substrates: 74 weighted individuals, 67 male and 7 female weights. Mud 45.5 male and 4.5 female; carnivore dung 21.5 male and 2.5 female; herbivore dung zero. Female proportion 9.46% overall, 9.0% mud, 10.42% carnivore dung. Authors report no detected sex-ratio difference between those two substrates. Fractional counts reflect their allocation rule, not literal fractional animals.
- Salt concentrations 1, 10, 100, 1000 and 5000 mM: counts 28,13,8,8,9. Low concentrations account for 41/66 (62.12%). Do not infer a monotonic dose response or exact optimum.
- Feeding among observed butterflies: 66/79 on salt versus 4/36 on pooled demineralized water/NH4Cl/urea/uric acid treatments. Descriptive fractions 83.54% vs 11.11%, ratio 7.52. Not an attraction-rate ratio or adjusted causal estimate. Some feeding occurred at ammonium and urea baits; earlier shorthand that these never attract/feed is too strong. No new inferential test because raw trial dependence, sampling effort, IDs and exposure are unavailable.
- These data justify separating sodium-mediated resource suitability from nitrogen enrichment and allowing females to visit. They do not establish that visiting females were receptive, that mating occurred or that frogs provided the substrate. Do not insert the 9.46% fraction directly as model f (fraction of all available females using a hotspot); it is the proportion female among bait visitors, a different denominator.

## Revised experimental priority (explicit decision)
Do not privilege foam nests solely because they are protein-rich. Carcass pathway is supported at the butterfly-family level; naturally spent foam nest, excretion-conditioned margin and tadpole legacy remain unverified for butterfly resource use. Keep Pieridae as the primary target and Nymphalidae/Lycaenidae as positive resource-use comparator groups. No intentional killing, collection of active nests, live bait or wildlife translocation. First phase is noninvasive observation of naturally occurring resources with ordinary moist controls. Compare resource classes at the same site/time, record substrate chemistry (soluble Na, K, conductivity, NH4, pH, moisture), background adult abundance and accessibility. Use time-lapse or repeated video to distinguish true feeding from landing, record individuals and sexes separately, assess female receptivity if feasible. Treat zero visitors as a monitored outcome, not absence inferred from citizen-science silence.
Only if adult Pieridae use and receptive female encounters are supported, move to randomized layout experiments and direct fertilization assays (v0.2). For natural carcass observations, a matched non-amphibian carcass or safe chemistry mimic can distinguish general carrion from frog-specific recognition. The causal claim of a natural frog-provided resource is not equivalent to species-specific chemistry. Prioritize the missing link rather than fitting another generic co-occurrence model.

## Status
G0 all-butterfly frog substrate use: supported by direct reports. G0 Pieridae frog substrate use: unverified. G1 aggregation relative to controls: qualitative literature support only for vertebrate carrion in pooled taxa. G2 mating initiation and G3 fertilization: unverified. No primary field dataset downloaded or GitHub Actions outcome verified in this update.
