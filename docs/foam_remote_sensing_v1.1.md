# AECO v1.1 — remote-sensing feasibility
2026-11-25. Satellite/archival aerial imagery can characterize potential breeding environments, not identify individual foam nests or Pieris mating/fertilization. Close-range imagery may detect visible nests with field validation. No raster or new field imagery acquired.

## Evidence
Miyata et al. (2026), Herpetology Notes 19:65–67, https://herpetologynotes.org/index.php/hn/article/view/315 : Table 1 and Fig. 1 (p.66) checked against page image. Seven records/cases; one refers to repeated years, not seven independent one-time nests. Five measured fossorial nests have short axes 49–89 mm and long axes 68–120 mm. Two reported burrows 40 and 165 mm deep. Exact localities not copied. These sizes are not a representative sample of arboreal nests. Same-species placement contrasts potentially useful; proposed competition release not established causally. No direct recipient use evidence.
General arboreal size 100–150 mm from Heibonsha encyclopedia. A Japanese pond study https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ceispapers/ceis23/0/ceis23_0_149/_article/-char/en reports heights 106–950 cm (mean 434 cm, n=148). Foliage and geometry limit overhead visibility.

## Direct extraction
Projected short-axis/GSD is nominal pixel width, not detector recall. Sentinel-2 10 m (https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/data-collections/copernicus-sentinel-missions/sentinel-2), WorldView-3 PAN 31 cm (https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2021/1030/i/ofr20211030i_ver1.1.pdf), and GSI ortho 20/40 cm (https://www.gsi.go.jp/KOUKYOU/BasicSurveys_Area.html) are subpixel for a 10 cm nest. Bright subpixel signals cannot reliably identify or enumerate individual nests; upsampling cannot restore missing observations. Five measured short axes require <=9.8 mm GSD for all to span 5 pixels, <=4.9 mm for 10 pixels. At 5 mm GSD 4/5 span at least 10 pixels. Planning heuristics only, no empirical cutoff.
Prioritize non-disturbing pole or telephoto RGB and optional authorized oblique UAV survey where safe. Check landowner/site permits and aviation rules https://www.mlit.go.jp/koku/koku_fr10_000042.html . Avoid downwash/shadow effects, occupied nests, excavation, touching, or wetting; stationary cameras for behavior. Calibrate at target plane. Label visibility, stage, scale, contrast and occlusion; include pale flowers, spider webs, damaged leaves, water glare and litter as hard negatives. Split validation by pond/date; compare with independent censuses and report visible-only recall, hidden nests, false positives per effort, and missing coverage. Non-detection is not absence.

## Landscape proxies
Kato et al. 2010, https://doi.org/10.1007/s13157-010-0103-1 (indexed abstract https://www.ricedata.cn/reference/papers.aspx?id=32952 ; DOI route inaccessible); Ministry of Environment project summary https://www.env.go.jp/earth/suishinhi/wise201103/H21-F-072-020.html describes previous Sado-wide predictions using egg-mass counts, forest cover within 1 km, paddy water depth and channel characteristics. General Sado habitat mapping is therefore not novel. New endpoint: accessible foam-area days and recipient encounter/fertilization conditional on independently monitored nests.
JAXA HRLULC 10 m 2020/2022/2024 v25.04 (registration required): https://www.eorc.jaxa.jp/ALOS/jp/dataset/lulc/lulc_v2504_j.htm ; GSI imagery for larger pond and canopy features https://www.gsi.go.jp/gazochosa/gazochosa40001 ; Dynamic World 10 m scene-level cover probabilities https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_DYNAMICWORLD_V1 ; agricultural reservoir inventory https://www.maff.go.jp/j/nousin/bousai/bousai_saigai/b_tameike/ichiran.html excludes many small woodland pools.
