# AECO v1.0 — Material accessibility as a gate between frog foam and butterfly reproduction
Date: 2026-11-25. Published aggregate reanalysis and prospective design; no direct adult Pieridae foam use, mating-site formation, or recipient fertilization verified.

## Research focus
Retain the primary system Zhangixalus arboreus × Pieris melete/P. rapae and the endpoint of at least one directly confirmed fertilized egg per initially unmated female over a specified horizon. Foam is a frog-built fertilization environment. Its use by adult butterflies is a missing upstream link, not an established interaction. Distinguish approach (appearance), contact/acceptance (chemistry), liquid uptake (material accessibility), residence and female/male encounter, completed mating, insemination and recipient fertilization. Do not assume feeding is necessary for a purely visual mating landmark.

## New primary evidence and quantitative benchmarks
1. Shigeri et al. 2021, Zoological Science 38:8–19, https://doi.org/10.2108/zs200113, Table 1 and Methods. The analyzed foam came from females laying in captivity without males, separated from eggs, frozen. For amino-acid composition, 10.02 mg wet sample was acid-hydrolyzed in 6 M HCl at 110°C for 24 h. Table 1 gives normalized residue counts, not free amino-acid concentrations. Eight essential amino acids listed by the authors sum to 404.1 residues per 1000 (40.41%); other essential residues may be unmeasured or altered, notably tryptophan. Cysteine 57.5 vs 14.3 in a previously published frog meat comparison (~4.02-fold); not a controlled feeding experiment. The printed row sum differs from 1000 because of rounding; retain raw values. Sodium citrate in Methods is added buffer, not a foam Na measurement. 22 proteins identified by homology, including inhibitors and defense-related candidates; activities and accessible amino acids cannot be inferred. Accessible surface Na, K, free amino acids, free sugars, UV reflectance and surface tension of target Japanese nests not found in this targeted search. Primary PDF extracted table and methods checked; screenshot requested but returned HTML, so no page-image confirmation. Article provides DDBJ DRA010399, PRJDB10049, SAMD00232153 and TSA ICQQ01000001–ICQQ01000022. These are sequence data, not metabolomics or optical spectra; do not present a proteome-based sodium prediction.
2. Cooper et al. 2005, Biophysical Journal, https://doi.org/10.1529/biophysj.104.046268, Fig. 2 and text: approximate surface tension 74 mN/m for water and ~50 mN/m at natural foam-protein concentration in Engystomops pustulosus (as Physalaemus pustulosus). Material also wetted a hydrophobic test substrate. Do not transfer these values to Japanese nests or to butterfly proboscis. Monteiro et al. 2023, https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms11040900, Table 1: foam fluid surface tensions 39.66, 45.34, 50.73 mN/m for three neotropical species vs water control ~71; proteins and carbohydrates differ among species. These are study-level summaries, not three independent species-level replications of a cross-species causal effect. No use of 16S library counts as chemistry replicate counts.
3. Lee & Lee 2014, Scientific Reports, https://doi.org/10.1038/srep06934, Fig. 4–5 and accompanying text: Polygonia c-aureum brush-tipped proboscis retains liquid after detachment from wet agarose, whereas P. rapae lacks this structure and loses liquid contact. Both feed from a liquid sheet; approximate areal uptake rates 0.454 and 0.239 mm²/s (ratio 1.90, P. rapae 47.36% lower). This is a real laboratory comparison, but not a population effect estimate, absolute volumetric flow, foam feeding test, or Pieris melete observation. No raw trials recovered.
4. Monaenkova et al. 2012, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284131/ and Lehnert et al. 2013, https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2013.0336 show liquid uptake from porous materials and regional wettability differences in proboscides. The 2014 comparison is the specific Pieris anchor. Some lepidopterans rehydrate dry substrates by saliva/excreted water (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5792911/); do not assume focal Pieris can rehydrate foam.
5. Boggs & Dau 2004, https://academic.oup.com/ee/article/33/4/1020/449418: Colorado Pieris napi as published preferred dilute salt baits; earlier v0.5 gives 1 and 10 mM reference concentrations. Japanese Papilio ion experiments, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-012-0976-3, measured preference for 10 mM and sampled natural puddling site Na up to 6 mM; not a Pieris-specific response function. No comparable target foam surface Na values found. Lamie et al. 2025 https://doi.org/10.1093/aesa/saaf007 notes limited support for direct reproductive benefit of sodium transfer. Retain a purely spatial encounter mechanism separate from nutritional effects.

## Physical availability: illustrative prediction, not calibration
For passive imbibition in a fixed cylindrical pore under ideal Lucas-Washburn assumptions, L²/t = r*gamma*cos(theta)/(2*mu). With equal r and mu, coefficient ratio for two liquids is (gamma_foam/gamma_water)*(cos(theta_foam)/cos(theta_water)). Using the published *other species* approximate 50/74 mN/m only to illustrate this trade-off, a water contact angle below acos(50/74) ≈ 47.5 degrees cannot be offset by improving wettability alone when viscosity is fixed. At water angle 60°, foam angle below about 42.3° could offset the lower tension. These are hypothetical contact angles and passive capillary conditions; actual proboscis has active suction, varying geometry, surface chemistry, and film replenishment. Foam may be dry, heterogeneous and viscoelastic. Neither the model nor multiplication of uptake and surface-tension ratios can predict real foam feeding rates or mating. Measure viscosity, surface tension, contact angle, liquid release and uptake directly.

## Falsifiable alternatives
- H_visual: appearance/UV contrast recruits oriented approaches even if surface dry and inaccessible. No necessary ingestion.
- H_chemical: measured soluble compounds change feeding acceptance conditional on approach and matched physical substrate.
- H_access: matched ionic and free nutrient concentrations yield different actual uptake rates when liquid-film accessibility changes. Predict larger wet-versus-dry penalty for P. rapae than a brush-tipped comparator such as P. c-aureum, but not a demonstrated species effect on foam.
- H_legacy: naturally abandoned foam retains accessible liquid or leachate after rewetting; no monotonic age assumption. Intact foam, retained residue, and underlying leaves/wet substrate are separately coded; runoff feeding is not direct foam contact.
- H_encounter: increased residency and spatial overlap of receptive females and males increases whole-array confirmed fertilization under limiting conditions; alternatively no effect or diversion. Nutrient physiology distinct from spatial encounter. Panorpa remains a candidate mating comparator, not a replacement for Pieris.

## Next empirical gate (priority over further general theory)
G0 natural observations as v0.7–0.9, before outcomes select matched ponds/branches; video with wide and close views. In addition to foam contact, record use of wet leaves or substrate below the foam with explicit geometry and background controls. Natural rain and dew can be recorded, never applied to active nests. No handling, removal, wetting, or sampling of occupied nests. Naturally abandoned material only after confirmed departure and local permissions/biosecurity; avoid precise public location.
G1 authorized laboratory characterization of naturally abandoned material: independent foam nests, matched adjacent leaves or wet substrates, rainwater and procedural blank; keep stage, dry mass, extraction volume, recovery and limits of quantification. Use accessible surface liquid/leachate and separately standardized extract if available, never equate extract with natural exposure. Quantify Na/K/Ca/Mg and chloride by validated ion methods, free sugars and free amino acids by calibrated chromatographic methods without hydrolysis, conductivity, pH, water content, liquid release per area, viscosity and surface tension where sufficient liquid, advancing/receding contact angle on standardized nonbiological proxy, spectral reflectance 300–700 nm under calibrated illumination. Record censored values, not zero. Sample-level ID, blanks, calibration and matrix spike recovery. Wetting treatments only on permitted naturally abandoned material or inert mimics in contained conditions.
G2 contained choice/feeding assay after cue characterization: independently randomize visual contrast (calibrated inert replicas), chemistry (target-derived measured mimic vs blank with same physical matrix) and accessibility (two physically measured film/release levels with same solution, area and nominal total resource). Verify rather than assume treatment separation, especially contrast, evaporation and surface chemistry. Use randomized position, independent insect IDs, repeated trials as within-ID; monitor species, sex, age, initial hydration and mating history. Compare P. rapae, P. melete where permitted and available, and P. c-aureum as a wet-surface-feeding reference, not as a known foam visitor. Include standardized positive liquid feeding control and blank no-insect evaporation controls; estimate corrected fluid mass or a validated harmless tracer in contained trials. Mouthpart contact and residence alone are not ingestion. Measure actual uptake and orientation separately. An inert surrogate can test sufficiency, but cannot establish that a natural foam has that property until chemically/physically matched. Do not expose wildlife to experimental chemicals in situ.
G3 proceed to concentrated/dispersed equal-cue spatial arrays only if direct Pieris use or verified visual response and evidence of compatible encounters. Follow initially unmated females, including nonmating and no-egg females, for direct fertilization assay validated with virgin and fertile controls (v0.2); hatching separate, missing follow-up explicit. If female arrival is not increased or mating is redistributed, do not claim a population fertility gain. Record direct frog embryo cost separately if studying mutual effects.

## Output and limitations
Transcribed published aggregate values and computed arithmetic, not new measurements. A simple physical scenario is labeled hypothetical. Direct searches for foam nest × Lepidoptera/Pieridae in English and Japanese yielded no verified new direct foam contact. Search is targeted, not systematic. No raw sequences or individual feeding data acquired; no new field footage reviewed. Japanese sodium and free nutrient chemistry, natural Pieris contact, mating and fertilization are unresolved. The critical next gate is authentic behavioral observation or physical/chemical characterization, not another parameter grid.
