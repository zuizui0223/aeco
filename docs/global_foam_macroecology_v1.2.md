# AECO v1.2 — Global macroecology of reproductive architecture
2026-11-25. Scope supersedes Sado-only habitat screening. No empirical global grid result yet; the aim is a global foam-producing frog trait/range/phenology panel linked to Pieridae activity, not a map of individual nests or a claim of fertilization.

## Primary data and why the trait is identifiable
Nunes-de-Almeida, Haddad & Toledo (2021), Salamandra 57:413–427: 2,012 anurans sampled in a detailed classification (71 anuran modes; 74 across amphibians). Source article https://zenodo.org/records/5205572/files/Nunes-de-Almeida_et_al-1470.pdf ; code descriptions https://www.salamandra-journal.com/SuppData/Nunes-de-Almeida_et_al-1470-SI.pdf ; original workbook listed in publisher directory https://www.salamandra-journal.com/salamandra/57-2021/57-3/ . Modes 44–64 explicitly describe froth; 65 is bubble and remains separate in the strict primary definition. Crosswalk records substrate and possible exposure, not actual exposed area; leaf/branch, ground and floating foam are potentially exposed, burrows sheltered, containers/depressions/constructed leaves/insect mounds variable. “Nest absent” in codes 44,45,58,59 does not mean froth absent. Multiple modes per species retained; missing or genus-level inference not accepted as confirmed nonfoam. The source classification is a historical incomplete sample, not all frogs.
Gould (2021) https://doi.org/10.3390/ecologies2010006 reports at least 19 genera in seven families under the taxonomy used then; do not apply foam to every member of those genera or treat families as independent evolutionary origins. A 2023 review https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2022.0141 documents variation and convergence. For Old World tree frogs, Meegaskumbura et al. (2015) https://doi.org/10.1111/zsc.12121 and the 2022 climate/island study https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-022-03292-1 already analyze mode and climate. General climate association alone is not novelty. A 2022 global life-cycle dataset https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-34474-4 uses six coarse categories and cannot serve as foam-specific labels. Use its sources/taxonomy and possible phylogeny for coverage checks, not infer foam from semi-terrestrial mode. Zhangixalus placement inventory https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blab143 can provide regional multimode validation.
Workbook URL was identified and clicked in the publisher listing; web retrieval does not support XLSX and local DNS resolution failed. No raw workbook bytes acquired, no count of foam species yet. scripts/global_foam_traits.py and workflow are an acquisition/schema gate; inspect actual header/row structure before explicit parsing. Workflow execution/result not verified here. Do not fabricate global occurrence or trait counts.

## Frozen global panel proposal
1. Assemble audited species-level foam traits (confirmed froth, bubble separate, confirmed nonfoam, unknown) and placement categories. Match taxonomic authorities explicitly (GBIF accepted names, amphibian authority, synonyms) with crosswalk and provenance; ambiguous matches unresolved. Use genus-level inferred traits only in a separately labeled sensitivity. Keep species with mixed placement as mixed; no assignment of fixed fraction of exposed nests. Validate regional species coverage, check missingness by family/realm, and compare results using evidence-confirmed traits only.
2. Spatial: equal-area 100 km cell primary, 50 and 200 km sensitivity; continental land across available data coverage, ecoregion/realm blocks and land fraction. Independently define native ranges from IUCN (license/approval required) or curated GBIF records; butterfly range alternative Daru (2024) https://doi.org/10.1002/ecy.4462 / https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.bzkh189h9 covers 10,372 species at 5 arcmin, map archive ~7.85 GB. No map file acquired yet. Do not assume all Pieridae are covered. Keep observation support and modeled range as different layers. Build potential-exposed-foam species richness / all trait-known frog species richness and strictly sheltered foam richness separately; unknowns remain missing. Neither richness nor fraction is nest density or exposed area.
3. Time: 2010–2021 primary overlap with JRC monthly water history (30 m, 1984–2021, https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/JRC_GSW1_4_MonthlyHistory); aggregate observed water/nonwater area and valid observation support within each equal-area cell, never treat no data as dry. Derive within-cell seasonal water dynamics and inundation onset; Landsat may miss small/covered ponds. Combine precipitation (GPM IMERG or ERA5-Land), temperature and vegetation activity (MODIS NDVI/EVI), tree-cover/land-cover and daylight. Freeze versions/masks after acquisition. These are habitat and activity covariates, not direct foam detections.
4. Frog timing: prioritize independently verified nest/egg or spawning observations (with taxon and date); breeding call records are an upstream breeding-activity proxy and adult sightings are not nest formation. If sufficient monthly counts by cell/year/species are unavailable, report the spatial trait/range map only, no invented reproductive phenology. Use paired sampling windows and observed effort or target-group background; no-effort bins missing, not zero. Butterfly timing: prioritize reviewed courtship and mating onset records; ordinary adult occurrence is a weaker flight-activity proxy. Do not equate adult activity or local mating with fertilization.
5. Define opportunity index only on valid supported month pairs: normalized monthly frog breeding proxy convolved with 0-, 14-, 21-day residue windows (sensitivity; not global measured persistence), times standardized recipient activity; evaluate overlap against null models retaining each taxon's seasonality, local climate, observation effort, species/range composition and spatial autocorrelation. For coarse monthly data, use no lag vs one-month lag as sensitivity, not a claim to resolve 14-day effects. Test lagged associations separately for exposed foam versus sheltered foam and verified nonfoam controls; environmental covariates include contemporaneous and lagged rain, warmth, vegetation and surface water. Require blocked holdout regions and family/clade-stratified comparisons. Do not simply shuffle across climates. For spatial richness compare conditional association with Pieridae vs other butterfly families, accounting for total frog richness, butterfly habitat, climate and effort; report alternative controls, no causal interpretation. Distinct positive hypothesis: exposed-foam potential has an incremental and stage-/lag-specific association beyond general breeding activity, with weaker/no association for sheltered foam or nonfoam. Alternative shared habitat/season, observation effort or recipient avoidance. No causal fertilization inference from this analysis.
6. Only after finding target behavior or obtaining standardized reproductive outcomes, fit an independent model of mating/confirmed fertilization per initially unmated female. Macro associations define encounter opportunity/hypothesis priorities, not a fertility surface. Report geography and season of candidate interactions; never label an overlap raster as actual foam-resource supply or mating probability.
